TEST 1 — generation as PAYLOAD (ADR 0023's rejected design)
   SELECT ... FROM payload_gen FINAL WHERE generation = 1   (want: 1 row, v=100)
   got: 0 row(s), v=0
   -> FINAL collapsed the two generations on key (k) and kept the newer one;
      the WHERE then discarded it. ADR 0023's objection reproduced exactly.

TEST 2 — generation LEADING the sort key (ADR 0034)
   SELECT ... FROM keyed_gen FINAL WHERE generation = 1     (want: 1 row, v=100)
   got: 1 row(s), v=100
   and the in-flight generation, asked for by name: 1 row(s), v=999
   -> (1,7) and (2,7) are different keys. FINAL has nothing to collapse.

TEST 3 — THE TRAP: FINAL over a normal VIEW is a SILENT no-op
   base table, FINAL, k=9                    -> 1 row(s), v=20   (correct: v=20)
   view WITHOUT final, reader says FINAL     -> 2 row(s), v=30   <-- WRONG, and silent
   view WITH final, reader says nothing      -> 1 row(s), v=20   (correct)
   view WITH final, reader ALSO says FINAL   -> 1 row(s), v=20   (correct; outer FINAL is inert)

   -> No error, no warning: FINAL on a view is dropped. Every pinned base
      view over a Replacing tier must therefore carry FINAL ITSELF, and
      downstream FINAL can no longer be trusted to mean anything.
