PHASE 7 — DIMENSION CHANGE. Late events flip a published interval's platform.

  Dimension attribution is the DOMINANT value among an interval's events
  (ADR 0008/0009). A burst of late events under a new platform outvotes the
  old one, so the re-derived interval moves to a different dimension tuple:
  every minute it covers must be RETRACTED from the old platform's user
  buckets and hour curves, and credited to the new one's.

PHASE 12 — CRASH MATRIX (Q8, ADR 0019). Kill the publisher at EVERY phase
           boundary, then run it again and prove full recovery each time.

  Sixteen injection points: after each phase mark AND after each heavy
  statement (before its mark) — the two ADR 0016 phases included. Three
  recovery shapes are exercised:
    claiming/consumed/batch  -> ROLLBACK. The intent row (phase 'claiming',
        written before any side effect) names the run; recovery deletes its
        consumed rows, drops its batch partition, marks it aborted, and the
        markings become claimable again. Before ADR 0019 a crash between
        the consumed insert and the claimed mark orphaned the batch FOREVER
        — with pending_sessions reading 0 (reproduced).
    claimed..users marks     -> RESUME from the phase marker, reusing the
        run's recorded build_version. Recomputing BV on resume made the
        prune delete the crashed run's own derivation (reproduced: the
        session vanished from all four tiers, every status green).
    *_stmt points            -> RESUME must decide whether the statement
        landed. The insert_deduplication_token does NOT decide it: a replayed
        INSERT SELECT into the shared delta table executed BOTH times on
        26.2.1.525 (system.query_log showed two QueryFinish rows, each with
        written_rows > 0, and the correction double-applied — caught by this
        matrix's first green-to-red run). Recovery now consults the server's
        query log for the negate/emit query_ids; every other statement is
        replay-safe by construction (pinned build_version + Replacing/idempotent).
  Each recovery must also outwait the dead holder's lease (TTL 6 s here).
  Every round appends one +30 s beat to the synthetic probe session and
  requires the published end to advance by exactly +30 s through the crash.

PHASE 13 — TWO PUBLISHERS AT ONCE (Q9, ADR 0019). The lease admits one.

  Without the lease this exact scenario was reproduced corrupting the tier:
  two publishers both claimed the same marking under different run_ids, both
  negated the SAME published contribution X and both emitted X-prime, so the
  served number landed at 2X'-X — one viewer became two. Both runs were
  green; nothing detected it; only a full rebuild could repair it.
